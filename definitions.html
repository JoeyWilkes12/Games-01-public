A few things to know before you compare.

A guide to the score, the strategies, and the limits of the recorded data.

The score counts every merge.

When two matching tiles combine, the new tile’s value is added to your score. The final score is the sum of these merge values throughout a game. It is different from the largest tile on the board.

2 + 2 → 4

This merge adds 4 points. Merging two 4s adds 8 more.

Four ways to choose a move.

The workbook labels its runs with the following strategy codes. These descriptions explain the method families; implementation and training settings were not recorded with each run.

Monte Carlo Mc
Estimates the quality of possible moves using random simulations. A move can be selected from the outcomes of those samples. The result depends on the simulation policy and budget.
Expectimax E
Searches a tree of player moves and chance events. At chance nodes, it combines possible tile spawns using their probabilities. Search depth and the evaluation function affect its decisions.
IDDFS graph search Igs
Iterative deepening depth-first search explores successively deeper limits. Graph-based state tracking can avoid revisiting duplicate positions. The workbook does not identify its search depth or evaluation rules.
Reinforcement learning Rl
Learns decisions or state values from experience and reward. Its performance depends on the model, training, and whether search is added at play time. Those details are absent here.

Compare like with like.

The dataset has 80 runs on a standard 4 × 4 board and 68 on a 5 × 5 board. Each strategy has 20 runs on the smaller board and 17 on the larger one.

A larger board can sustain longer games. Compare strategies within one board before interpreting their raw scores. The dashboard keeps the two boards separate by default.

Mean score
Add the recorded scores and divide by the number of runs. A very high score can pull the mean upward.
Median score
The middle score after sorting the runs. With an even number of runs, average the middle two. Use it alongside the mean to understand the typical recorded outcome.
Score spread
The dots show individual outcomes. A wide range indicates variation in these runs; it is not a confidence interval or a claim of statistical significance.

Know what the data cannot tell us.

There are no recorded timestamps, seeds, move counts, maximum tiles, compute budgets, or elapsed times. Some notes mention reaching a tile, but notes are incomplete. We cannot calculate win rates, speed, or efficiency from these fields.

The “Full Name” field is inconsistent. Strategy names are resolved from the workbook’s code mapping. All 148 rows have valid positive scores and are included.

These results describe this collection of recorded games. They do not establish a universally best 2048 algorithm.
